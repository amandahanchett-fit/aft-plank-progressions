Plank Progressions for the Army Fitness Test

Few events on the Army Fitness Test reward patient practice as quickly as the plank. You simply hold one rigid line from head to heels while a grader watches your posture. The plan below turns a wobbly half minute into a steady, confident hold over six weeks of short, repeatable sessions.

Start by learning the position. Place your forearms on the ground with elbows under shoulders, feet about hip width apart, and your body in one straight line from head to heels. Squeeze your glutes, pull your ribs down and keep your neck long. Most failed planks come from hips that sag or rise, so check your line in a mirror or on a short video.

Phase one covers weeks one and two. Do five holds of twenty to thirty seconds with a minute of rest between them, three days per week. Stop each hold the moment your form breaks, because sloppy time does not build the skill. Add dead bugs and bird dogs on two other days to teach your trunk to brace while your limbs move.

Phase two covers weeks three and four. Build to three holds of forty five to sixty seconds. Add side planks of twenty to thirty seconds each side, which strengthen the obliques and stabilise the hips. Practice breathing slowly through the nose and mouth instead of holding your breath, since a held breath ends the hold early.

Phase three covers weeks five and six. Use longer holds of ninety seconds to two minutes, two or three sets, and add variety such as shoulder taps, body saw rocks, or a plank with feet on a low step. These raise tension in the trunk and teach you to stay tight when you feel fatigue.

Phase four is test preparation. Do one full length practice hold per week under test conditions, with a partner who watches for hips, head and hands. Stop the other trunk work two days before the real test so you arrive fresh. Avoid hard leg days right before it too, since tired glutes make the hips drop.

On test day, get into position calmly, set your breathing before the clock starts, and pick a focus cue such as glutes tight, ribs down. If your shoulders burn, shift your attention to your breath and keep the body line. Do not chase seconds in the first minute; protect form so the hold is counted until the end.

Track your progress in a log. Write the date, hold length and how it felt. After each practice test you can use AFT Calculator Army Fitness Test Score Calculator to see how many points the hold earns for your age band, and decide whether the plank is worth more training time than the other events.

A final reminder: strong trunk muscles help the deadlift, the drag carry and the run too. Plank training pays off across the whole test, so treat it as part of your weekly routine rather than a last minute add on. Stay patient, add time slowly, and keep every hold clean.
